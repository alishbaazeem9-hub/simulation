From WhatsApp chaos to structured workflow — how much time does the portal free?
This simulator quantifies the operational impact of replacing LAAM's fragmented WhatsApp + Google Sheets pipeline with a custom PRaaS portal. The goal is not revenue extraction — LAAM charges brands and talents 0% commission. PRaaS is a complementary service. The impact is measured in time saved, coordination eliminated, capacity unlocked, and brand GMV enabled. Every projection is grounded in real LAAM data: Aiman Murtaza's operator logs (18 hrs/campaign), Maliha Noor's mismatch interviews (33% rejection rate), and the March 2026 campaign results from PROGRESS.xlsx.

The core logic of this simulation
LAAM's PRaaS portal does not generate value by extracting a commission — it charges 0%. The value is in operational efficiency: less time per campaign, fewer mismatches caught late, more brands served per season, faster payments, and better-executed campaigns generating more GMV for brands. Every lever below is calculated from time units, then translated into meaningful productivity gains. The data anchor is Aiman Murtaza's operator log (18 hrs/campaign) and Maliha Noor's mismatch interview (33% rejection).
1
Campaign capacity with portal
Shorter cycle time means the same operator can run more campaigns without hitting a coordination ceiling. If the cycle compresses from 21 days to 9.8 days, capacity increases by 2.14×. We cap this at 1.8× to be conservative — the operator also needs buffer for quality reviews and new brand onboarding.
Capacity multiplier = min(cycle_days_before / cycle_days_after, 1.8)
Campaigns / yr (portal) = (campaigns / month × 12) × adoption% × capacity_multiplier
Base case: (25 × 12) × 70% × 1.8 = 378 campaigns / year vs 300 today
2
Lever 01 — Coordination hours reclaimed
Aiman Murtaza confirmed ~18 hours per campaign of coordination: managing WhatsApp groups (25+ at peak), updating Google Sheets manually, calling brands to re-confirm briefs, chasing payments, and tracking content approvals. The portal's structured intake form, Kanban board, and automated status notifications compress this to ~6 hours. No WhatsApp groups remain after Gate 3.
Hours freed / yr = (hrs_before − hrs_after) × campaigns_per_year
Productivity gain % = (hrs_before − hrs_after) / hrs_before × 100
Base: (18 − 6) × 378 = 4,536 hours freed / year · 67% productivity gain per campaign
3
Lever 02 — Mismatch time eliminated
Maliha Noor confirmed 33% of campaigns hit a budget-talent mismatch — wrong tier, wrong brief, or budget insufficient for the talent requested — discovered only after hours of outreach. Currently this triggers a full rework cycle: brief rewrite, new talent search, re-negotiation, and re-scheduling. The portal's budget firewall catches this at the intake form, before any time is invested in outreach. We estimate each prevented mismatch saves ~8 hours of rework.
Mismatches prevented / yr = (campaigns_before × mismatch%_before) − (campaigns_after × mismatch%_after)
Rework hours avoided / yr = mismatches_prevented × rework_hrs_per_mismatch
Base: (300 × 33% − 378 × 8%) × 8 = (99 − 30) × 8 = 552 rework hours avoided / year
4
Lever 03 — Campaign capacity unlocked
The capacity constraint is not talent availability — it is operator coordination overhead. Currently, 18 hrs/campaign × 6 brands/cycle = 108 operator-hours per campaign cycle. With the portal at 6 hrs/campaign, the same 108 hours serves 18 brands. This is not a projection — it is arithmetic. Peak Season 2026 data confirms the problem: 32 brands wanted PRaaS, only 6 were executed. PKR 38.75M in brand budgets went unserved because of coordination bottleneck, not demand.
Brands served / cycle = min(brands_onboarded, round(6 × hrs_before / hrs_after))
Additional brands = brands_after_cycle − 6 (current)
Base: min(22, round(6 × 18 / 6)) = min(22, 18) = 18 brands per cycle · +12 additional brands served
5
Lever 04 — Payment speed gained
Today, payment release takes 3–7 days per campaign because proof is shared via WhatsApp screenshot, manually verified, and subject to dispute about WHT (14% withholding tax, which brands frequently don't budget for, discovered mid-campaign). The portal's escrow wallet holds payment proof in-system, WHT is disclosed at the New Brief stage, and release is triggered automatically on content approval. Target: <24 hours (1 day).
Payment days freed / campaign = pay_delay_before − pay_delay_after
Total campaign-days freed / yr = days_freed × campaigns_per_year
Payment speed improvement % = (pay_before − pay_after) / pay_before × 100
Base: (5 − 1) × 378 = 1,512 campaign-days freed / year · 80% faster payment release
6
Lever 05 — Brand GMV uplift enabled (0% LAAM take)
The portal enables better-executed campaigns — right talent, validated brief, right timing, no inventory mismatch. Better execution drives brand GMV uplift. LAAM takes 0% of this GMV. The value for LAAM is brand loyalty, portfolio retention, and platform stickiness, not revenue extraction. We use Bareen × Hira Faisal (+141%, PKR 2.64M incremental) as the conservative benchmark and apply it to the fraction of portal campaigns that achieve meaningful uplift.
Brand GMV enabled = campaigns_per_yr × uplift_hit_rate% × avg_incremental_GMV_per_campaign
Base: 378 × 45% × PKR 1,200,000 = PKR 204M brand GMV enabled / year · LAAM take: PKR 0
Why we excluded Sajal Aly from the base case
Urge Pret × Sajal Aly generated +652% GMV (PKR 2.76M → PKR 20.73M). This is a top-0.1% A-tier celebrity result that is not replicable across LAAM's 32-brand portfolio. Using it would inflate the GMV projection dishonestly. The base case uses PKR 1.2M incremental GMV — approximately 45% of what Bareen × Hira Faisal actually achieved (PKR 2.64M) — which is genuinely conservative. The simulator allows you to slide upward if you believe the portfolio can consistently match Bareen-level results.

Why the Kainat Faisal campaigns failed — and how the portal prevents this
The two Kainat Faisal kaftaan campaigns declined −59% and −91% in March 2026. This is not a talent failure — kaftaan is a seasonal product that was already declining in February. The problem is that this campaign should never have been approved to go live in March. The portal's inventory gate (blocks go-live if stock is insufficient or declining) and brief validation layer (checks product seasonality trend before budget is committed) would have caught this at submission — saving the coordinator's time, the brand's brief-writing effort, and preventing a poor result from being attributed to the talent. This is exactly the coordination failure that Scenario C solves.
